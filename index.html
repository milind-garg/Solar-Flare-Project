Dual-Instrument X-Ray Synchronization

Aditya-L1's payload architecture combines low-energy thermal diagnostics with high-energy non-thermal spectroscopy from the Sun-Earth L1 Lagrange point.

SoLEXS
URSC Bengaluru · Solar Low Energy X-ray Spectrometer
1–8 Å / 1.5–15 keV

Equipped with twin Silicon Drift Detectors (SDD1 & SDD2). Measures soft X-ray thermal coronal emissions produced by flare plasma heating. Primary basis for GOES-equivalent classification (A, B, C, M, X).

PRL Ahmedabad · High Energy L1 Orbiting X-ray Spectrometer
10–160 keV

Employs Cadmium Zinc Telluride (CZT) and NaI(Tl) scintillators. Detects non-thermal bremsstrahlung from relativistic electron precipitation during the impulsive magnetic reconnection phase.

Solar Physics Foundation

The Neupert Effect in Flare Dynamics

In solar flare physics, the hard X-ray impulsive burst typically peaks before the soft X-ray thermal peak. As non-thermal electrons collide with the dense chromosphere, chromospheric evaporation fills coronal loops with hot plasma, accumulating thermal soft X-ray emission:

FSXR(t) ∝ ∫t₀t FHXR(t') dt'  ⟹  dFSXR/dt ∝ FHXR(t)

By tracking HEL1OS impulsive bursts during the SXR rise phase, the pipeline identifies the acceleration peak with a median Neupert lag of +134 seconds, providing advance warning of the thermal maximum.

01
Pre-Flare Energy Storage
Coronal magnetic shear builds in active region. QPP oscillations appear in loop structures.
02
Impulsive Phase (HEL1OS Peak)
Reconnection accelerates particles downwards. Non-thermal bremsstrahlung spikes in HXR (10–160 keV).
03
Thermal Gradual Peak (SoLEXS Peak)
Chromospheric evaporation fills loops. Soft X-rays peak 2–15 minutes later, dictating GOES class.
